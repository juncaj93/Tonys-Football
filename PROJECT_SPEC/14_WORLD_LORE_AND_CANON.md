# 14 — World Lore and Canon

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Canonical world specification  
**Primary question:** What is true about the world of Tony’s Pizza, and what should remain unexplained?

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the fictional world surrounding the league.

It governs:

- Tony’s Pizza Parlor;
- the underground casino;
- manager basements;
- recurring NPCs;
- canonical objects;
- historical league mythology;
- recurring phrases;
- world boundaries;
- ambiguity.

This document describes what exists.

Implementation, probability, cooldown, dialogue, and feature behavior belong in other files.

---

## 2. The World

Tony’s Pizza is a neighborhood pizza parlor that appears to have operated for decades.

It is located in a fictionalized Metro Detroit setting.

The exact address should remain unstated unless later intentionally created.

The building feels familiar, worn, and personalized.

It contains:

- a main dining area;
- a counter;
- booths;
- sports memorabilia;
- a newspaper display;
- a collectible machine or equivalent;
- hallways or doors leading to manager basements;
- a suspicious back door;
- an underground casino.

The building’s interior geography is not fully rational.

That is acceptable.

---

## 3. Tony’s Pizza Parlor

The parlor is the heart of the application.

It is where:

- users arrive;
- Tony appears most often;
- the latest Tuesday Slice is displayed;
- recent league activity is visible;
- routes to collectibles, basements, and casino are discovered;
- seasonal decorations appear;
- rare events may interrupt ordinary activity.

The parlor should feel lived-in rather than staged.

Canonical details may include:

- red-and-white checkers;
- wood paneling;
- old Detroit football memorabilia;
- arcade carpet;
- neon signs;
- paper menus;
- a CRT;
- faded photographs;
- Tony’s handwritten notes;
- inexplicable access doors.

The space should accumulate subtle history over seasons.

---

## 4. The Underground Casino

The casino exists beneath or behind the pizza shop.

Tony appears to operate it.

No one has formally explained:

- when it was built;
- who licensed it;
- how large it is;
- why it has more rooms than the building should allow;
- why Tony is always present.

The casino uses fictional league tokens.

It is colorful, arcade-like, and more playful than realistic.

Launch games:

- blackjack;
- slots.

Roulette exists as prepared future space but remains hidden until enabled.

The casino is part of the fictional world, not a real-money platform.

---

## 5. Manager Basements

Every active manager has a permanent basement.

The basements are simultaneously:

- personal rooms;
- collectible galleries;
- character spaces;
- social destinations;
- extensions of the impossible building.

No one explains how ten basements fit beneath the parlor and casino.

Each basement may contain:

- shelves;
- wall displays;
- character area;
- basement theme;
- interactive props;
- seasonal decorations;
- entrance animation.

Basements persist across seasons.

---

## 6. Tony

Tony is the owner of the parlor and the central recurring character.

Canonical traits:

- middle-aged;
- slightly balding;
- apron over Detroit football-style jersey;
- cigarette behind ear;
- pizza owner;
- gambler;
- Lions fan;
- deeply invested in the league;
- appears in many roles.

Tony’s role is intentionally ambiguous.

The world should not explain whether:

- he writes every line of the Slice;
- he controls the collectible machine;
- he owns every basement;
- there are multiple Tonys;
- he sleeps;
- he has legal authority.

Tony’s full character rules are defined in `12_TONY_PERSONALITY_BIBLE.md`.

---

## 7. Bapple

Bapple is primarily a collectible and lore concept.

The Bapple Tree is an especially important manifestation.

Canonical associations:

- Nick is obsessed with Bapple;
- Bapple may appear as a collectible;
- Bapple may be displayed or interacted with in basements;
- the concept should remain slightly absurd and underexplained.

Do not create a detailed biological origin or overexplain the name.

Bapple gains power through familiarity and recurrence.

---

## 8. Portable Sauna

The portable sauna is associated with Brandon.

It may appear as:

- collectible;
- basement prop;
- Gazette callback;
- rare interactive object;
- visual joke.

It should not appear constantly.

Its existence is funnier than an explanation of why it exists.

---

## 9. Cottage Inn Delivery Guy

The Cottage Inn Delivery Guy is a rare NPC.

Canonical event:

- bursts through a door;
- carries a gun as an exaggerated private-group visual gag;
- shouts: “WHERE’S BRANDON?!”

This event must remain rare.

The world does not explain:

- why he is armed;
- what Brandon did;
- whether he actually has a delivery;
- why Tony allows this.

Because the imagery involves a weapon, use must remain tightly controlled, clearly cartoonish, private, and non-threatening in presentation.

---

## 10. Clooner

Clooner is a rare singing NPC.

Canonical line:

> “Farmingtonnn, Farmington Hillllls.”

The performance should be brief, recognizable, and unexplained.

Clooner should not become a routine singer or music system.

---

## 11. Feasel

Feasel is a rare NPC associated strongly with Brandon’s stories.

Visual canon:

- dirty-blonde hair;
- exaggerated but recognizable presence;
- should feel like a figure from a story who somehow became real.

Feasel may appear through “Feasel Fables.”

Feasel Fables are:

- exaggerated;
- fragmentary;
- treated as accepted lore;
- not necessarily fully explained;
- best used sparingly.

Do not create a definitive Feasel biography unless the commissioner later adds it.

---

## 12. Berardo

Berardo is a historical or exiled figure.

Canonical treatment:

- quietly gone;
- absence is acknowledged indirectly;
- no detailed explanation;
- may appear in archival jokes or background references;
- not an active manager.

The mystery is the joke.

Do not invent a scandal or cause.

---

## 13. Topouzian Brothers

The Topouzian brothers are part of league mythology.

Canonical premise:

- shared or linked history;
- recurring suggestion that one did the work while the other received credit;
- remembered more as a combined myth than separate active characters.

This should be framed as group lore rather than a factual accusation.

They are not active managers.

- hidden sign;

The phrase should feel like remnants of a campaign nobody fully remembers.

“Just 18” is a rare, intentionally ugly soccer-player NPC or collectible concept.

Use carefully.

Canonical qualities:

- phrase-based recognition;

## 18. League History as World History

Real league events become part of the world.

Examples:

- championships;
- infamous trades;
- weekly records;
- rivalries;
- first legendary pulls;
- major casino stories;
- Tuesday Slice receipts;
- manager turnover.

These events should be preserved in structured history.

The fictional world may frame them dramatically but cannot change the facts.

## 19. The Newspaper

Official title:

# Tony’s Tuesday Slice

Official subtitle:

> Fresh Out of the Oven

The publication is physically represented within the parlor.

It is not titled “The Gazette” in user-facing branding unless used generically in technical discussions.

Tony is not a separate newspaper-editor persona.

The Slice belongs to the world and carries Tony’s voice.

## 21. What Must Remain Ambiguous

Do not explain:

- impossible building dimensions;
- Tony’s omnipresence;
- casino legality within the fiction;
- Berardo’s exile;
- Cottage Inn motive;
- the origin of “Join the revolution”;
- Bapple biology;
- the full truth of Feasel stories;
- how manager basements connect.

Mystery is a product asset.

---

## 22. Canon Authority

Canon comes from:

1. this document;
2. dedicated character documents;
3. approved dynamic-lore records;
4. persisted real league events;
5. commissioner additions.

AI-generated prose is not automatically canon.

A funny line becomes canon only when intentionally promoted.

---

## 23. Canon Changes

When adding lore:

- identify whether it is real league history or fictional world detail;
- record source;
- assign people;
- define restrictions;
- define active date;
- determine whether it replaces prior canon;
- preserve old published history.

Do not silently rewrite previous issues.

---

## 24. World Boundaries

The world may be surreal but should remain coherent.

Allowed:

- impossible architecture;
- recurring NPCs;
- animated collectibles;
- Tony appearing in multiple roles;
- exaggerated private-group comedy.

Avoid:

- random magic systems;
- unrelated fantasy creatures without setup;
- constant fourth-wall breaks;
- large external lore unrelated to the league;
- explanations that make the world less funny;
- uncontrolled expansion into a generic RPG.

---

## 25. Acceptance Criteria

World canon is working when:

- users recognize the parlor as a consistent place;
- Tony remains central but not overexplained;
- basements feel permanent;
- rare NPCs remain surprising;
- old managers remain historical, not active;
- real league events remain factually accurate;
- fictional framing never rewrites history;
- mysteries remain unresolved;
- Bapple, Feasel, Cottage Inn, Clooner, and other lore are recognizable without exposition;
- new AI prose cannot silently create canon.

---

## 26. Final Principle

Tony’s Pizza should feel like a place that has accumulated stories rather than a place designed to display jokes.

The world does not explain itself.

The league already knows.
