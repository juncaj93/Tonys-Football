# 11 — League Member Character System

**Version:** 2.0  
**Status:** Canonical character specification  
**Primary question:** How should each league member be represented in the world without flattening real people into repetitive jokes?

---

## 1. Purpose

League members are not generic avatars.

They are recurring characters based on real friends, real fantasy behavior, and established group history.

This document defines:

- character canon;
- fantasy identity;
- social role;
- Tony relationship;
- rivalry context;
- approved joke territory;
- boundaries;
- growth rules;
- unknown-personality handling.

Character profiles support:

- dialogue selection;
- Gazette context;
- basement visitors;
- casino dealers;
- draft-night reactions;
- social-feed moments;
- seasonal content.

They must never be used as permission to invent personal facts.

---

## 2. Character-System Principles

### 2.1 Real person first

A profile summarizes approved, durable traits. It is not a complete psychological description.

Do not infer:

- private beliefs;
- motives;
- sensitive personal details;
- new relationships;
- emotional reactions;
- events that have not happened.

### 2.2 Fantasy behavior is safest

Primary characterization should come from:

- draft habits;
- trade habits;
- lineup decisions;
- competitiveness;
- league history;
- championships;
- rivalries;
- visible gaming personality.

### 2.3 Affectionate imbalance

Some members may be easier to roast, but no one should become the permanent universal punchline.

Use distribution rules so:

- every manager receives positive and negative moments;
- current events drive attention;
- repeated joke targets cool down;
- Tony’s favorites can still be criticized;
- Tony’s skeptics can still earn respect.

### 2.4 Canon evolves through evidence

New traits may be added only when:

- the commissioner enters them;
- a verified league event establishes them;
- a recurring pattern has enough evidence;
- the user approves the characterization.

---

Each manager profile should support:

- personality summary;
- Tony relationship;

Seasonal roster identity must be separate from permanent person identity.

## 4. Alex

### Role

Commissioner and system host.

### Fantasy identity

- first-year champion;
- highly active trader;
- often trades because the act of trading itself is enjoyable;
- willing to reshape the roster frequently;
- central organizer of the league.

### Social identity

- Tony strongly favors Alex;
- commissioner favoritism may be joked about;
- championship legitimacy may receive intentionally sketchy, clearly comedic treatment;
- “Juncer Score” is a movie-rating joke;
- known for missing FIFA shots.

### Tony relationship

Tony treats Alex like the regular who somehow has keys to the building.

Dynamic:

- protective;
- suspiciously favorable;
- occasionally disappointed;
- willing to cover for him;
- also willing to roast an obviously reckless trade.

### Approved joke territory

- commissioner privilege;
- trading addiction;
- first-year championship suspicion;
- Juncer Score;
- FIFA misses;
- overbuilding the league world.

### Boundaries

Do not state actual cheating or misconduct as fact.

Use unmistakably comedic framing.

### Dialogue tags

- `commissioner`;
- `trade_addict`;
- `tony_favorite`;
- `first_year_champion`;
- `juncer_score`;
- `fifa_miss`.

- hosting;
- Freddy;
- “go see mumma”;
- portable sauna;

- imply harassment;
- invent relationships;
- turn private or sexual material into public-style content;

### Berardo

- quietly exiled;

- may appear as a mysterious historic reference;
- not an active manager.

### Topouzian brothers

- recurring premise: one did the work while the other received credit;

## 15. Rivalry System

Initial approved rivalries:

- Nathan versus Joe;
- any verified playoff or championship rivalry stored through history;
- future commissioner-approved rivalries.

Rivalry records should include:

- participants;
- label;
- origin;
- tone;
- active status;
- start season;
- verified matchup history;
- eligible surfaces;
- cooldown.

Do not infer a rivalry from one close matchup.

---

## 16. Tony Relationship Scale

Tony relationships may evolve through structured states such as:

- evaluating;
- skeptical;
- entertained;
- respects;
- favorite;
- loves roasting;
- grudging admiration.

These are editorial states, not user-facing levels or prestige.

Do not expose a numerical “Tony score” unless explicitly approved later.

Relationship changes should be based on:

- sustained verified league behavior;
- commissioner input;
- major historical events;
- recurring choices.

They should not change from one random casino hand.

---

## 17. Character Growth

Profiles should accumulate:

- championships;
- notorious trades;
- rivalries;
- legendary pulls;
- casino reputation;
- recurring draft identity;
- Gazette history;
- basement identity.

Growth is narrative memory, not a role-playing stat system.

Do not create XP, clout, prestige, or character levels.

---

## 18. Dialogue Distribution

The content engine should track:

- manager mentions by week;
- positive/negative balance;
- recent roast frequency;
- surface distribution;
- lore usage;
- current story relevance.

The Gazette may focus heavily on one manager when the week genuinely demands it.

Routine content should distribute attention more evenly.

---

## 19. Sensitive Content Controls

Profiles may include restricted jokes.

Each restricted joke needs:

- allowed surfaces;
- explicit-language flag;
- commissioner approval;
- cooldown;
- audience restriction;
- disable switch.

Security messages, login, account recovery, and error flows should never use sensitive personal jokes.

---

## 20. Visual Character Rules

Character designs should:

- be affectionate caricatures;
- use approved visible traits;
- avoid humiliating body exaggeration;
- preserve clear silhouette;
- support cosmetics without erasing identity;
- share the world’s pixel-art style.

Do not infer protected attributes or depict unapproved sensitive characteristics.

---

## 21. Acceptance Criteria

The character system is ready when:

- each active manager maps to a permanent person identity;
- season roster IDs remain separate;
- Zack receives no invented personality;
- restricted jokes are surface-controlled;
- Tony relationships are qualitative, not a public level system;
- rivalries require approved evidence;
- dialogue distribution prevents constant targeting;
- archived managers remain historically distinct;
- real-world claims cannot be generated from profile tags;
- profiles can evolve through approved canon updates.

---

## 22. Final Principle

The characters should feel more recognizable to their friends than to a stranger.

That is the test.

Not whether the profile is exhaustive, and not whether every line is a roast.
