# 01_VISION_AND_DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md

Version: 2.0

## 1. North Star

Tony's Pizza Fantasy is a persistent social game built around one private fantasy-football league.

It should make league members say:

> "I can't believe our fantasy league has its own video game."

Sleeper remains the functional fantasy platform. Tony's Pizza becomes the digital clubhouse where the group catches up, laughs, gambles tokens, collects items, and preserves its history.

## 2. Player Fantasy

Opening the site should feel like walking into **Tony's Pizza Parlor**, a nostalgic 1990s/early-2000s pizza diner somewhere in Metro Detroit.

Inside are:

- red vinyl booths and old Pizza Hut energy
- league members appearing around the restaurant
- fantasy news and standings integrated into the environment
- a newspaper rack containing Tony's Tuesday Slice
- vending and claw machines leading to loot systems
- a suspicious back door glowing with music and neon, leading underground
- Tony inexplicably performing nearly every job

The experience should be familiar, funny, slightly shady, and warm—not a Detroit tourist theme and not a generic neon casino.

## 3. Core Product Pillars

### Fantasy Truth

All entertainment begins with accurate league data. Scores, transactions, standings, and historical facts must be trustworthy.

### Weekly Storytelling

Tony's Tuesday Slice turns the week's 4–5 most interesting moments into a quick, funny, animated read. It focuses on stories, not an exhaustive recap.

### Permanent Collecting

Managers spend seasonal tokens chasing permanent sports memorabilia, inside-joke artifacts, cosmetics, auras, animations, and basement themes.

### Social Identity

Every manager has a customized character and permanent basement. The collection itself is the flex; no artificial level or clout score is needed.

### Controlled Chaos

Casino games, rare NPC visits, social activity, and Tony's commentary create unexpected moments without overwhelming the core fantasy experience.

### League Memory

Each season adds Gazette issues, trophies, records, collectibles, and lore. The site grows more valuable because the group has lived in it longer.

## 4. The Core Engagement Loop

```text
Real fantasy performance
        ↓
Verified rewards and story candidates
        ↓
Tony Tokens
        ↓
Loot boxes, casino play, and year-end auction
        ↓
Permanent collectibles and customization
        ↓
Basement visits and social activity
        ↓
More anticipation for the next fantasy week
```

Casino play can redistribute or risk tokens, but it must not replace fantasy performance as the main progression source.

## 5. Why It Is Different From Sleeper

Sleeper answers:

- What is my lineup?
- Who won?
- What are the standings?
- Which players scored?

Tony's Pizza answers:

- What was actually hilarious this week?
- Whose trade came back to haunt them?
- Who escaped through absurd luck?
- What story is becoming league lore?
- Who pulled something legendary?
- What new content just opened downstairs?
- What does our league remember years later?

## 6. The Tone

The tone is:

- nostalgic
- competitive
- rude in an affectionate friend-group way
- playful and occasionally ruthless
- comfortable with curse words when appropriate
- grounded in real personalities and events

It should never feel:

- corporate
- sanitized into blandness
- generically "quirky"
- like ChatGPT writing sports copy
- like every manager is being forced into one caricature

## 7. Tony's Role

Tony is a middle-aged, slightly balding pizza-shop figure with Mario-style energy, a Lions jersey under his apron, and a cigarette behind his ear.

His official role is intentionally unclear.

He may be seen:

- making pizza
- sweeping the underground casino
- introducing a side bet
- shaking hands on a wager
- reacting to a loot pull
- sitting in somebody's basement
- appearing in the background of almost any location

The recurring joke is: **does Tony do everything?**

Tony is not the literal newspaper editor. Tony's Tuesday Slice is an in-world publication that uses his voice, quotes, opinions, and presence.

## 8. The Long-Term Player Experience

After several seasons, a basement should communicate a manager's story without a numerical status score.

A visitor should see:

- championship history
- signed football memorabilia
- rare inside-joke collectibles
- old seasonal items
- character cosmetics and equipped aura
- animations earned or pulled over the years

The emotional value comes from recognition: the item means something to this group.

## 9. Product Restraint

The world should feel rich, not crowded.

Use:

- rotating characters instead of everyone always appearing
- rare NPC events instead of constant cameos
- 4–5 Gazette stories instead of dozens of sections
- a few basement display categories instead of furniture micromanagement
- two polished casino games at launch instead of a large arcade
- meaningful activity-feed events instead of constant notifications

## 10. First-Season Success

The first season is successful when:

- managers voluntarily open the site each Tuesday
- Tony's Tuesday Slice becomes a group ritual
- fantasy facts are trusted
- managers care about their token balance
- legendary pulls cause league conversation
- people visit one another's basements
- the commissioner can operate the site without repetitive manual work
- the world feels ready to grow next year

## Final Design Principle

Do not make fantasy football more complicated.

Make the league feel more alive.
