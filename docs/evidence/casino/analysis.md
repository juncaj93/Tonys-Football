## 1. Blackjack, under the approved rules (R2, R4)

Rules modelled: hit · stand · natural · bust · dealer draw · push · dealer stands on all 17 ·
natural pays 3:2 · no split, double, surrender or insurance · one fresh 52-card deck per hand.

| Model | House edge | EV per unit | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infinite deck (continuous shuffle) | **2.5438%** | -0.025438 | exact enumeration, no sampling |
| **Single deck — the shipped model** | **2.0306%** | -0.020306 | 20M hands, σ/√n = 0.000221 |

The single-deck figure is the one to use. Its 4σ band is ±0.0882 percentage points, so the two models differ by 0.5133 points — a real difference, not noise.

Natural frequency is the mechanism: a single deck deals one 4.827% of the time against 4.734% with infinite cards, because an ace you hold is one of four rather than one of thirteen.

## 9. Wager size against weekly income — the lever that actually moves this

A median manager earns **115 tokens a week** (150 × 50% of weeks + 400 × 10%), which is 1955 a season and about 9.8 boxes. Every result above follows from one ratio: what a week of play costs, against that.

| Buttons (slots / blackjack) | Cost per week: casual | regular | heavy | Heavy as share of income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 / 20 | 5 | 26 | 99 | **86%** |
| 10 / 40 | 11 | 53 | 198 | **172%** |
| 20 / 80 | 21 | 105 | 396 | **344%** |

At the 10-token slots button one box costs **134 spins** of expected loss. That single number is the whole economy question: it is large enough that a casual player never notices and small enough that a determined one converts their collection into entertainment.
